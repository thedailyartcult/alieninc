About A-SAN

ASEAN Superiority Aerospace Navigator — a technical intelligence platform for advanced aerospace and military systems analysis.

Mission

A-SAN provides defense companies, researchers, and authorized partners with structured technical intelligence on aerospace and military platforms. The platform consolidates open-source technical data, verified imagery, and analysis tools into a single searchable catalog — enabling faster research, better engineering decisions, and deeper understanding of advanced systems.

What A-SAN covers

The catalog spans ten equipment categories, from manned aircraft and unmanned aerial vehicles to missile systems, electronic warfare assets, and ground vehicles. Each entry follows a standardized ontology that makes cross-platform comparison and structured querying possible.

  • Aircraft — manned fixed-wing and rotary-wing combat and support platforms
  • UAVs — reconnaissance, attack, and loitering unmanned aerial systems
  • Air-launched munitions — air-to-air, air-to-ground munitions and guided bombs
  • Rocket and missile weapons — ballistic, cruise, and tactical missile systems
  • Sea-launched cruise missiles — naval cruise missile platforms
  • EW assets — electronic warfare, jammers, and signal intelligence
  • UGVs — unmanned ground vehicles for combat, logistics, and reconnaissance
  • Armored vehicles and equipment — main battle tanks, IFVs, APCs
  • Automotive vehicles — military trucks, logistics, and support vehicles
  • Small arms — infantry weapons and related systems

Data principles

Every entry in A-SAN is built from publicly available sources, cross-referenced for accuracy, and tagged with a confidence level. We maintain a full citation trail so that any claim can be independently verified. Corrections and updates are versioned — prior data remains accessible for longitudinal research.

Data sources

A-SAN aggregates factual technical data from approved public and licensed sources. Each catalog entry carries the exact source URL it was fetched from, so any data point can be traced back to its origin. The deep scanner respects every source's robots.txt and terms of service; only data printed on the source page is recorded, with no inference or completion.

  • Army Recognition — armyrecognition.com: defense product catalog and news (active crawl)
  • Designation-Systems.net — designation-systems.net: Andreas Parsch's directory of US military rockets and missiles (air-launched munitions, sea-launched cruise missiles, rocket and missile weapons)
  • Missile Threat (CSIS) — missilethreat.csis.org: CSIS Missile Defense Project "Missiles of the World" database (sea-launched cruise missiles, rocket and missile weapons)
  • Modern Firearms — modernfirearms.net: Maxim Popenker's encyclopedia of firearms (small arms)
  • Milrem Robotics — milremrobotics.com: Estonian UGV specialist — THeMIS, HAVOC RCV, Vector RCV, Type-X (UGVs)
  • FAS (Federation of American Scientists) — man.fas.org: 790+ structured equipment pages — land vehicles, naval vessels, aircraft equipment (501(c)(3) nonprofit)
  • Wikipedia — en.wikipedia.org: List of military electronics of the United States (1552 entries) + individual EW system pages with spec infoboxes. Creative Commons CC BY-SA 4.0 — freely republishable with attribution
  • War & Sanctions (GUR of Ukraine) — war-sanctions.gur.gov.ua: Ukrainian Defence Intelligence portal tracking the aggressor's military-industrial complex — Russian UAV systems with declared characteristics and related manufacturers (UAVs, Russia)
  • Defense Express — en.defence-ua.com: Ukrainian defence-analytics outlet — weapons & technology coverage with manufacturer specification tables (all categories)
  • Baykar — baykartech.com: manufacturer-direct fact sheets for Bayraktar TB2/TB3, Akinci, Kizilelma and loitering munitions (UAVs, Turkey)
  • Army Guide — army-guide.com: land-forces equipment database — artillery, armored vehicles, trucks with property/value specification tables
  • GlobalSecurity.org — globalsecurity.org: military systems library — munitions, aircraft systems, ground equipment (fact extraction per site content signals)
  • Covert Shores (H I Sutton) — hisutton.com: submarine, midget-submarine, SDV and UUV analysis with specifications (Naval vessels)
  • Seaforces — seaforces.org: US Navy and international naval fleets — ship classes, hulls and naval weapon systems with specification blocks (Naval vessels, weapons systems)
  • Weaponsystems.net — weaponsystems.net: structured weapons-system factsheets
  • Military Factory — militaryfactory.com: aircraft by-country listings
  • Google Patents — patents.google.com: public patent pages
  • Espacenet (EPO OPS API) — official European Patent Office API (credential-gated)
  • USPTO — official PatentsView / PEDS APIs (credential-gated)
  • Janes — licensed research-desk exports (paid licence, CSV import only)

Source content remains the property of its respective owners. For sources that are all-rights-reserved (no Creative Commons licence), A-SAN records only factual data printed on the source page — designation, dimensions, weights, ranges, country of origin, manufacturer — plus a short factual description, and links back to the original page. The CSIS Missile Threat citation string is preserved verbatim in each relevant entry for clean attribution. Requests to republish longer excerpts or imagery from any source must be directed to that source's rights holder.

Who A-SAN is for

A-SAN is designed for defense engineers, technical researchers, analysts, and organizations working on countermeasures, platform analysis, and threat assessment. Public visitors can browse the catalog overview; verified partners gain access to full technical profiles, drawings, and research tools.
