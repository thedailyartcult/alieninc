01. What the guardian does

We watch. We verify. We do not operate.

Alien Inc. is a separate, independent checker. We do not run the data centers we review. That separation is the point.

Our work is to detect signs of sabotage in existing systems, track data center projects being built around the Philippines, and help verify that each facility is safe and recoverable. The records we care about are practical ones: financial registries, essential communication history, and the operating records a facility needs in order to restart after a failure.

What we do not do matters as much. We do not sell cloud space. We do not take a share of the facilities we review. When we find a problem, we document it in plain terms and report it through agreed channels. Trust is the product. Independence is what makes it real.

Sovereign Design Goal · 25-Year Design Life

A country that names its risks earns its investors.

The Philippines faces typhoons, earthquakes, floods, and outages. That is stated openly, because it shapes the work. In a crisis, state technical teams and facility owners should not have to improvise. The vaults are designed to be their fallback. On grid power with solar-diesel-battery redundancy, serviced quarterly, our vaults are designed to re-instantiate financial registries, essential communication history, and operating records from durable media back into working systems. This fallback is the design target. Contracts and independent audits are part of the program roadmap.

02. How the work is phased

Concrete steps, each with something to show.

Three phases in Philippine pesos. Each phase ends with a visible result: a published record, a working verification routine, or a built facility. Nothing vague. Nothing eternal.

Phase 01 · ₱0 – ₱3M

Inventory and baseline checks

  • Published facility inventory: A maintained list of data center projects around the Philippines — location, build stage, and stated capacity — compiled from public and owner-provided records.
  • Baseline safety checklist: One written standard covering physical safety, backup power, backup copies, and recovery steps. Every later review uses the same list.
  • Verification terminal: The Genesis Protocol terminal on alieninc.tech — a direct, in-browser demonstration of how a stored record is checked and restored. No intermediaries. No foreign dependency.
Phase 02 · ₱25M – ₱80M

Verification routine and forward station

  • Registration and working agreements: Freeport or PEZA-registered presence, permits, and written review agreements with facility owners. Paper before concrete.
  • Working review station (200–300 sqm): A hardened office and laboratory above PSA institutional build rates (₱16,771/sqm), where review equipment, reference copies, and test rigs are kept.
  • Independent power (200–500 kW): Grid connection plus solar-diesel-battery backup with UPS. Sized from proven Philippine microgrids (₱73M per MW at Tablas, 2019).
  • Regular review cycle: Repeated site reviews against the Phase 1 checklist, with findings documented and shared through agreed channels.
Phase 03 · ₱180M – ₱450M

Long-term archive layer (secondary)

  • What it is: A cut-and-cover archive vault (500–800 sqm) holding long-term recoverable copies — the secondary layer behind the guardian work, not the headline.
  • Honest construction: Rock excavation with dewatering and waterproofing, priced against DPWH excavation rates plus contingency. Seismic detailing to the National Structural Code of the Philippines, with independent structural review.
  • Power and upkeep: Dual feeds, diesel standby, battery ride-through, and cooling sized to actual load, with a 10-year operating and replacement reserve. Serviced quarterly. Nothing runs decades without service.
  • What success looks like: A sealed, audited copy of essential records that can be read back on standard equipment — verified by test restores, not by assertion.
03. Where the work sits

Two study sites for the archive layer.

The guardian work happens wherever facilities are built. The long-term archive — the secondary layer — is being studied at two inland rock sites. Both sit inside established economic zones with written agreements and outside review. That footing matters more than the rock itself.

Archive study site · Bukidnon

The Bukidnon Plateau (Mindanao)

High interior volcanic plateaus. Old basalt, away from the western Pacific typhoon corridors and above coastal flooding. Studied as the first archive site because the ground is stable and the tenure can be put in writing — long-term zone agreements with outside arbitration, so the terms do not shift with each administration.

Archive study site · Tanay

Tanay Formations (Sierra Madre)

Granite and limestone mass in the central Sierra Madre. Heavy mountain cover and distance from the coast. Studied as the second archive site so the long-term copies never depend on one place or one local office. Same principle: written tenure, outside arbitration, published terms.

05. Why the guardian can be believed

Separate books. Separate interests.

Alien Inc. holds seven operating companies — covering capital, risk, consulting, security review, media, culture, and legal support. The guardian unit keeps separate accounts, takes no revenue from the facilities it reviews, and publishes its methods. Outside auditors are part of the plan from the start.

Check 01 // Intake

Take in the records

Financial registries, essential communication history, and the operating records a facility needs in order to restart — received under written custody, never mixed with other business.

Check 02 // Common standard

One list for every site

Every facility is measured against the same written checklist in standard file formats — ordinary tables any engineer can open without special software. The same standard everywhere is what keeps reviews honest.

Check 03 // Tested copies

Prove recovery by restoring

Long-term copies sit in the archive layer on tape and optical media. Each copy counts only after a test restore on standard equipment. A backup nobody has restored is a rumor.

Plain files, readable for decades

Records are kept as plain, documented tables that open on standard equipment — no special licenses, no single vendor. State technical teams can read back financial registries and communication history under any future administration, because the format never belonged to one supplier.
