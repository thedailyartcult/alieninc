01 The Mandate

Most groups our size rent their security from a vendor and hope it holds. The Alien.Inc group chose to own it. CENTRA exists so that every company under Rousseau — a consultancy founded in 1969, a newsroom founded in 1926, and everything acquired since — runs behind the same walls, the same watch, and the same standard.

Why internal

An outside vendor optimizes for its contract. An internal division optimizes for the group — its people, its deals, its reputation.

Why one platform

Seven companies with seven toolsets means seven blind spots. One operating picture means a finding at any company raises the floor for all.

Why it matters

The group's business is trust — capital, counsel, and acquisitions. A single breach at any entity is a breach of the whole.

03 Service Lines

Three lines of defense, one team.

Everything CENTRA does for the group rolls up to three internal service lines — each with its own queue, its own metrics, and its own owner inside the division.

Service 01 / EVM

Exposure & Vulnerability Management

The scan engine never stops. Every internal service, cloud posture, and internet-facing surface is continuously assessed, deduplicated, and ranked — so each company's IT lead gets a short list of what to fix this week, not a thousand-row export.

  • Attack-path context — findings scored on whether they're actually reachable from the internet or a phished laptop.
  • Ranked remediation queues — owners, deadlines, and verification rescans built into every ticket.

05 Prioritization

Ranked by what can hurt the group — not by volume.

A 9.8 on an unreachable lab box ranks below a 7.0 on the system that processes payroll. Every finding that enters the queue is scored against three factors drawn from the asset graph — so the group's limited IT hours always land on the exposures that matter.

The result: of roughly 41,000 raw findings a year, about 300 reach a human queue. The rest are closed as unreachable, compensated, or accepted — with the reasoning on record.

×

Reachability

Can an attacker actually get to it — from the internet, a guest network, or a phished session?

×

Exploitability

Is exploitation happening in the wild, is a public exploit available, how reliable is it?

×

Business Impact

What does this asset do for the group — client data, deal flow, payroll, publishing, donors?

06 Response

An incident, hour by hour.

A real pattern from the group's incident log, anonymized. The same five steps run every time — the difference is how fast they run.

T+0:00

Detect

Impossible-travel sign-in flagged on a KMT analyst account. Session telemetry pulled automatically.

T+0:11

Triage

Duty analyst confirms the session is anomalous against travel schedule and device fingerprint.

T+0:26

Contain

Session revoked, account suspended, mailbox rules frozen. Lateral movement checks across the tenant.

T+4:40

Eradicate

Credential reset, device reimaged, OAuth grants audited, persistence checks clean. Account restored.

D+5

Review

Written timeline to the company's leadership; detection rule and travel-notification control shipped.

Median containment, rolling 12 months — 5.7 hours against a 6.0-hour internal SLA
07 Operating Principles

How the division works.

P-01

Internal by design

No external tenants, no outside customers, no conflicting incentives. The console, the scan engine, and the SOC serve the seven group companies and nothing else.

P-02

Findings, not noise

A scanner that pages people for unreachable CVEs trains them to ignore it. CENTRA's queue is short on purpose — everything in it is reachable, exploitable, and owned.

P-03

One baseline for everyone

The foundation handling donor data and the consultancy advising clients run on the same controls, the same MFA policy, and the same patch SLA. No exceptions by seniority.

P-04

Measure what matters

Time to contain, critical exposure age, MFA coverage, findings closed per week. If a metric doesn't change a decision, it doesn't go in the board report.

09 Engagement Model

How a group company comes under coverage.

The same four steps ran for every company in the group — and run for every company the group acquires, starting the day the deal closes.

01

Asset Inventory

Everything the company owns — domains, tenants, endpoints, cloud accounts — discovered and reconciled into the group asset graph.

02

Baseline Scan

Full authenticated assessment against the group baseline. First prioritized queue delivered to the company's IT lead within ten days.

03

Continuous Monitoring

Sensors and detections live; the company joins the 24/7 watch floor and the weekly exposure cadence.

04

Quarterly Review

Metrics, incidents, and open risk reviewed with company leadership and rolled up to Rousseau.

Intercompany engagements are documented and billed as formal services — ICT-001 (security review of client deliverables for KMT) and ICT-002 (cyber diligence support for Exosphere) — $184K annually, reviewed each fiscal year by the holding company.
