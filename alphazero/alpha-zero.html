Alpha Zero

Alpha Zero is a decision-simulation engine that branches real events into thousands of alternate timelines — starting with the stock market — to show you the paths you didn't take, and what they would have cost or earned you.

The Engine Quote Limits & Metrics

Alpha Zero: Every decision, branched

Alpha Zero runs on the same instinct that powers life-simulation games — a self-deterministic finite-state machine that lets you live out a decision more than once. But where those games generate outcomes from randomized tables, Alpha Zero starts from what actually happened.

Give it a domain with a measurable, real-world outcome — a stock, a portfolio, a company's trading history — and Alpha Zero treats every past decision point as a fork, not a fact.

Unlike relational-data simulators, which model a life as a chain of pre-scored events, Alpha Zero's engine is only given the rules and the record. It isn't told what the "right" call was.

It then plays the record forward millions of times, branching a new parallel timeline at each decision point. Through this process, the system learns which of the untaken paths would have actually outperformed the one that happened.

Point it at a single stock decision and it can generate thousands of divergent timelines in minutes. Point it at a portfolio, and it maps the whole tree of what could have been.

The output isn't a prediction — it's a map of near-misses. It shows you how close history came to going the other way, and how far off the winning branch actually was.

“The past isn’t a single story — it’s just the one branch that happened to win. Alpha Zero is how we go back and read the ones that didn’t.”

Patrick Neil A.
Founder, ALIEN.INC

Limits and valid metrics

Branching a decision was only ever the proof of principle for Alpha Zero.

The engine is only as honest as the data it starts from. It can't branch off an event that was never recorded, and it can't account for the truly unprecedented — a black-swan event has no prior branch to learn from.

So every output is scored, not just generated. A branch is judged against measurable, real-world benchmarks: the outcome delta against what actually happened, a confidence score based on how many independent branches converge on it, and a backtested accuracy rate against decisions we already know the ending to.

That means Alpha Zero never claims certainty. It reports a distribution of outcomes and how tightly they cluster — the tighter the cluster, the more the "parallel universes" agree with each other.

Today, that's applied to a single stock decision at a time — feed it a site, a ticker, or a trade, and it hands back the branches that beat it and the ones that lost worse.

And that's just the beginning. The same branching engine that works on a stock decision works on any choice with a measurable before and after — which is most of them.

Simulate every path. Trust the one that wins.
